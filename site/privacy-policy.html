Privacy Policy

flock — last updated 08-09-2026

What flock is

flock is a private tool built by one person for their own use. It schedules and publishes videos to that person's own YouTube channel and TikTok account. It runs only on the author's own hardware and is reachable only on the author's private network.

Use of YouTube API Services

flock uses YouTube API Services to upload videos to the author's channel. That use is subject to the YouTube Terms of Service. Google's handling of data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

Use of TikTok's APIs

flock uses TikTok's Login Kit and Content Posting API to publish videos to its author's own TikTok account. Content published this way is subject to TikTok's Terms of Service and Community Guidelines. TikTok's own handling of data is described in TikTok's Privacy Policy.

flock uses what it receives from TikTok for one purpose: posting the videos its author chooses, at the time its author chooses. It does not read the account's videos, followers, statistics or messages, and it presents no TikTok content anywhere.

What flock accesses

From Google, flock asks for:

  • youtube.upload — uploading videos to the author's channel
  • youtube.readonly — reading the channel's own videos and their public counters
  • youtube — only if enabled, for adding a video to a playlist

From TikTok, flock asks for:

  • user.info.basic — the account's identifier, display name and avatar, so flock can show which account a post will go to
  • video.publish — posting a video directly to that account

Before each post, flock also reads what TikTok calls creator information: the account's display name, which audiences it may post to, whether comments, duets and stitches are switched off in its settings, and the longest video it may post. That is used only to build the post the way TikTok requires, and only the most recent copy is kept, replaced on the next read.

flock does not read, collect or store information about any other channel or account, any other user, or any viewer.

What flock stores, and where

Everything flock stores stays on the author's own equipment:

  • the video files, or references to where they are kept
  • the text written for each video: titles, descriptions, tags and publishing settings
  • the chosen release times
  • confirmation of what was uploaded and where
  • a log of actions taken in flock
  • a name for each browser that uses it, stored in that browser under flock.device
  • the Google refresh token, in a configuration file
  • TikTok's access and refresh tokens, in the same configuration file
  • the most recent creator information read from TikTok, as described above

Data leaves the author's machines only to publish a video, to Google or to TikTok, and only while that is happening. Between the author's own machines it travels over a private network.

How long it is kept

Everything flock stores stays until its author deletes it. Nothing expires on its own, nothing is copied anywhere else, and the tokens are kept until they are revoked or replaced.

Cookies and device data

flock sets no cookies and uses no analytics, telemetry or tracking of any kind. This website is a set of static pages and sets no cookies either.

Sharing

flock shares no data with anyone. There are no third parties, no service providers, no advertising, and no sale or transfer of data.

Deleting data

The author can remove anything flock holds by:

  • deleting the entry in flock
  • deleting flock's data directory
  • deleting the configuration file that holds the tokens
  • revoking flock's access to the Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions or security.google.com/settings/security/permissions
  • revoking flock's access in the TikTok app, under Settings and privacy, then Security and permissions, then the list of connected apps; that stops every further request, and the stored tokens stop working
  • deleting a published video on YouTube or TikTok itself; flock keeps no copy of what those services hold
